OWASP Top 10 For Agentic Applications 2026 and Agentic AI Governance and Security

November 1, 2026

OWASP (pronounced “Oh-Wasp”) has a cute logo that looks like this:

OWASP was founded in 2001 as the Open Web Application Security Project out of a mailing list of professionals supporting each other on the security concerns of the dial-up era of the internet.

Over time, OWASP has rapidly evolved to address security concerns not just with the Web, but also with IoT, mobile, cloud, APIs and now AI. In 2023, OWASP rebranded to Open Worldwide Application Security Project.

OWASP is an important organization in cybersecurity that is well known for its Top 10 reports. Each Top 10 is a free, ranked list of the ten most critical security risks in one area of technology.

This year’s 2026 is the first year OWASP has published a Top 10 for Agentic AI

OWASP GenAI Security Project. (2025, December 9). OWASP Top 10 for agentic applications for 2026. OWASP Foundation. https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/

As I learn about the security vulnerabilities related to agentic AI, I thought useful research would be to look at the OWASP top 10 and how the leading governance and security platform Zenity works to mitigate these risks. Surprised to learn that the Zenity CTO collaborated on writing the 2026 OWASP top ten - so we should take this with a grain of salt.

Below is a summary of the top 10 and how a platform for Agentic AI governance and security could defend against the vulnerabilities described in the OWASP top 10

The agents themselves, their memory and their connected data

ASI06: Memory & Context PoisoningFalse information gets planted in the agent’s memory or connected data (RAG) and quietly shapes later decisionsInspects what the agent writes to memory and how it uses context, then stops poisoned memory from steering its actionsDefend
